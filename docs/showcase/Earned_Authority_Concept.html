Motion has to be earned

You were right: the beam between proof and what the machine may do is in perpetual motion, and the ceiling is a dial someone drags. This is the reframe — authority the fleet has to earn, that a human has to grant, and that a single breach can take away instantly. No engine changes yet; this is the thinking.

Aligned, illustrative — NIST does not certify AI systems Concept — pressure-tested before any build Grounded — mapped to the real engine primitives

The problem, named exactly

The beam moves every frame because the spotlight PA’s evidence ratchets stage-by-stage and quick-completing PAs get swapped under it — motion for free, nothing earned frame-to-frame. And the org-wide ceiling — how high the machine may ever act — is literally FLEET_CEIL[maturityBand(s.maturity)], where s.maturity is a slider you drag. So authority today is set, not earned. Two meters and a slider read as “someone turned it up,” which is precisely what an executive — and a State auditor — should not accept.

The correction — the one idea the rest hangs on

Evidence can revoke authority automatically. It can never grant it automatically.

A named human grants authority. Evidence only makes a class eligible — and can take authority away the instant it degrades.

This is the guardrail a green-lights dashboard destroys. If the screen says “9 of 9 gates met — click to promote,” the human owns the accountability but the machine made the decision. So promotion is a human governance act that can veto even when every metric is green; only revocation is automatic. That asymmetry is the whole trust story — a system you can trust with authority is one that can lose it without asking.

Earning is slow, quiet, human-sealed

A sustained, case-mix-representative, recency-weighted record makes a class eligible. Then a named human ratifies — attestation sealed to the ledger. Nothing on screen “fills up into” a promotion.

Losing is instant, automatic, visible

One qualifying breach — a fairness signal, a replay failure, a drift alarm, an override spike — drops the ceiling, freezes promotions, seals a revocation receipt, and triggers a look-back on actions taken since the breach. No human needed to lose it.

The eligibility gates — necessary, never sufficient

Green gates make a class eligible to be considered; they do not promote it. Each is corrected for a gaming attack the coalition found. Authority is per class × per population — never one org-wide number.

🎯

Representative track record

Enough sealed, resolved determinations — stratified by complexity/acuity, and the banked set must mirror the population it will act on. Fix: authority earned on easy cases can’t be spent on hard ones.

🔁

Decision reproducibility (real replay)

Re-run the model/rule on the same pinned inputs and match the recorded decision. This is not the hash chain — hash re-derivation only proves the record wasn’t edited. Fix: a real replay harness; tamper-evidence ≠ reproducibility.

⚖️

Fairness — multi-axis, human-signed

Intersectional (race × disability × language × age), risk-adjusted, with four-fifths as one input. Never auto-cleared — an MD/equity officer signs, as the engine’s own §1557 ticket already does (A1/HITL). Fix: a scalar can’t clear this gate.

🕵️

Blinded human concurrence

On a risk-weighted sample (over-weighting high-harm / near-threshold), a reviewer blind to the agent’s recommendation concurs. Fix: kills anchoring bias and the easy-case blind spot.

🖐️

Override rate — with a dead-man’s switch

Overrides stay low and reviewers stay engaged. A suspiciously-low override rate is its own alarm (rubber-stamping), not a win. Fix: low override reads two ways; pair it with engagement.

📈

Stability & drift; independent validation

Version pinned, inputs in-distribution, no drift alarm — validated by a party independent of the model builder. Gates can’t all be self-measured by the system that’s earning. Fix: adds the missing external challenger.

— then, and only then —
✍️

Human ratification — a different kind of gate

A mandatory final AND-gate: a signature and seal, not a filling bar. It can veto with all gates green, and the ratifier’s rationale is sealed to the ledger (reuse the engine’s proposeOutbound/closeCase human-attributed pattern). This is where accountability actually sits.

Honesty ledger — real vs proposed (corrected)

Real today
  • evLevel ratchets per-txn; execAuthority = min(evLevel, ceiling), adverse floored to A1.
  • Fleet ceiling from a maturity slider (FLEET_CEIL[maturityBand]) — the thing we’re reframing.
  • authUnlockTick marks “authority rose”; spotlight holds a PA until it completes.
  • Hash-chain tamper-evidence (ledgerIntact/verifyEntry) — integrity, not decision reproducibility.
  • §1557 fairness screen at A1/HITL; typeCounts; per-record NIST tag.
Proposed (this concept)
  • Earned ceiling per class × population; slider demoted to a what-if scenario overlay.
  • Eligibility gates + banking + recency decay; a real replay harness for reproducibility.
  • Human ratification as a sealed, vetoable governed act (promotion never automatic).
  • Auto-revocation wired to fairness/drift/override/replay + look-back/recall; a global human kill-switch; a sealed, time-boxed break-glass override.
  • Cold-start shadow → validate → provisional grant; independent-validator gate; hysteresis + dwell to stop thrash.
