# Reconciliation & Drift Hardening — Adversary Findings (R1 domain-fidelity + R2 negative-space)

Role: reconciliation & drift adversary reviewing the US-healthcare-payer platform for DEPLOYMENT
robustness. READ-ONLY: findings only, no source modified.

Reasoning: tree-of-thought, one branch per reconciliation boundary (record-vs-projection, outbox-vs-
projection, claims golden-thread, 834 membership truth, identity, terminology currency, financial). The
lens is not "does the transform work once" but "when two stores that are supposed to agree silently
diverge in production, is there a JOB that DETECTS it, and a JOB that RECONCILES it — or does the drift
leak forever, invisible?"

## Headline

The platform has excellent *construction-time* correctness (idempotent upserts, per-member FIFO, E9 fail-
closed holds, rebuild-from-replay proofs) but almost no *standing reconciliation*. The primitives that
would detect and repair drift EXIST as pure functions and classes — `OutboxSweeper.sweep`,
`replayToStore`, `projectClaimsWithIntegrity`/`holdOrphanClaims`, `decideBindingCurrency`,
`registry.dueForRefresh` — but **nothing schedules or wires them in production**. There is no background
worker, no cron, no `setInterval`, no relay that (a) subscribes the C2 backbone to the graph projector,
(b) runs the outbox recovery sweep, (c) re-drives held orphan claims when the Claim lands, or (d) scans
value-set bindings for version drift. A drop, an orphan, a merge, or a stale value set therefore produces
a divergence that no automated check will ever surface. Most findings below are one facet of that single
gap: **the reconciliation code is present and tested, but un-run.**

## Cross-cutting observations

- The dominant pattern is **dormant reconciliation**: `OutboxSweeper.sweep`, `replayToStore`,
  `projectClaimsWithIntegrity`/`holdOrphanClaims`, and the currency deciders are all built, tested, and
  exported, but **no scheduler, worker, or live consumer invokes them in production**. The platform proves
  reconciliation is *possible* in tests and never *performs* it at runtime. Wiring a background job
  runner (relay + sweep + rebuild-compare + orphan re-drive + currency scan) closes the largest share of
  these findings at once.
- The system is strong on **duplicate**-delivery reconciliation (idempotent upserts, eventId idempotency
  stores) and weak-to-absent on **gap** reconciliation (dropped events, missing sequences, absent Claims,
  omitted 834 members). At-least-once delivery needs BOTH; only one half is covered.
- **Full-file / snapshot truth** (834 membership, coverage spans, duplicate members) is entirely
  unmodeled: every adapter reasons per-record, so any drift that only shows up as a DIFFERENCE BETWEEN two
  full states (this file vs last file, this roster vs held roster) is structurally undetectable.
- **Financial reconciliation** (REC-07) is the one boundary with no primitive at all — no 835 ingest, no
  three-way tie-out — so it is a build gap, not merely an un-run job.

## Summary

14 findings. Severity: 3 Critical, 6 High, 5 Med. The un-run-reconciliation root (REC-01/02/03) gates the
rest; scheduling a projection consumer with checkpointing, the recovery sweep, and a rebuild-and-compare
job is the highest-leverage hardening.
